About

Who I am and where I'm headed.

I came to cybersecurity through intrusion detection: how a system decides something is an attack, and what it costs when it's wrong. At Kanto Gakuin University I spent two academic years testing machine learning models on the CICIDS2017 dataset, then continued my studies in the US. Now I'm working toward a role as a SOC analyst, where accurate detection, real-world threat analysis and informed incident response meet.

  • A two-year research effort that fundamentally shaped my approach to cybersecurity. Driven by a strong interest in IDPS, I implemented and evaluated multiple machine learning algorithms against the CICIDS2017 dataset to understand how ML can address real-world network security challenges, and where it falls short.

    What I did

    • Implemented four ML algorithms: SVM, Naive Bayes, Decision Tree and k-Nearest Neighbors
    • Ran binary classification experiments with precision and recall as the primary metrics
    • Wrote about 10 Python source files, each over 1,000 lines
    • Identified gaps between theoretical performance and real-world false positive rates on normal traffic
    • Refined the experiments through continuous trial and error over two academic years
    • Gained a realistic view of the challenges of running IDPS in operation
